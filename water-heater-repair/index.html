No hot water or a leaking tank at night

A water heater fails in one of two ways. It stops making hot water, which is annoying, or it leaks, which can ruin a finished basement before morning. A leak that comes from a fitting or the relief valve is often repairable. A leak from the bottom of the tank usually means the steel has rusted through.

If you see water pooling, turn off the cold water supply to the tank and switch the power off at the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot. Then call us. Do not open the drain valve on a tank that is hot and leaking, because the water can scald.

No hot water at all

On an electric tank this is often a tripped breaker, a failed upper element or a bad thermostat. On gas it may be the pilot or thermocouple.

Water that runs lukewarm

A burned-out lower element or a sediment-packed tank cannot heat the full volume.

Popping or rumbling

Sediment on the tank floor traps steam bubbles. Flushing sometimes helps, and a very old tank may be past it.

Dripping relief valve

The valve may be doing its job after a pressure spike, or it may have failed and need replacing.

Repair or replace: how the decision works

Age and condition decide this more than the symptom does. A tank around the ten year mark has seen most of its life, and a repair on an old tank can be followed by a second failure a few months later. A newer tank with a bad element is a straightforward repair.

You hear a clear price before work starts. A new thermostat and a replaced gas control valve are not close in cost, so we quote after we diagnose.

The biggest factors are:

Fuel typeGas and electric tanks use different parts and different safety steps.
Tank ageAn older tank may need extra fittings or a code update while we are there.
AccessA heater in a tight closet or under a stairway takes longer to service.
Time of dayNight, weekend and holiday visits can cost more than a weekday visit.

Sudden water damage from a failed tank may be covered by your homeowner's policy, but the heater itself usually is not. We can describe what we found in writing for your claim.

Hard water, old tanks and Massillon basements

Massillon's public water comes from Aqua Ohio's Massillon Division, which pulls from wells in the Tuscarawas Valley sand and gravel aquifer. Ask your water utility for the current hardness figure. Whatever it is, minerals settle on the bottom of a tank over the years and make the burner or lower element work harder.

Draining a few gallons from the tank once a year clears some of that sediment, and replacing the anode rod protects the steel lining. Many Massillon heaters sit in basements with floor drains close by, which helps. A water softener slows the scale if your water is hard.

The Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, sets standards for relief valves and discharge piping on water heaters, and we follow the maker's instructions for every part we fit.
