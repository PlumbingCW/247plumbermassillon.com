Where basement water comes from

Water enters a basement three main ways. Groundwater rises through the floor or the wall joint after heavy rain. Surface water runs toward the house and seeps through foundation cracks. Sewer or drain water backs up through a floor drain or a toilet.

Each has a different fix. A new drain tile and sump pit handle groundwater, grading and gutters help surface water, and a backwater valve or a repaired sewer line handles backups. Spending money on the wrong one wastes it.

Water at the wall joint

Seepage where the wall meets the floor is typically groundwater.

Water rising in the floor drain

Gray or dark water from a floor drain is a sewer issue.

White mineral crust

Efflorescence on walls marks where water has passed through the masonry.

Musty smell

A damp basement grows mold even when the floor looks dry.

Massillon basements and the river valley

Downtown Massillon flooded often enough that the city finished a levee and viaduct program in 1951. That work eased the river threat. The groundwater that rises under a house in a wet season is a separate matter, and it is why many valley basements keep a pump running each spring.

In Perry Township, the 2025 comprehensive plan notes 100-year floodplain in the northwest and stormwater runoff problems in the northeast and north-central areas. Homes there and in low parts of Jackson Township near the Tuscarawas River benefit from a pump with a backup.

What waterproofing cannot do

Plumbing-side fixes control the water that reaches the pit and the drains. They do not seal a cracked foundation wall, replace failing gutters or regrade a yard that slopes toward the house. If you hire one trade for everything, ask who is responsible for each part, because a perfect sump pump will not stop water that enters above the floor.

A good plan has layers. Gutters and downspouts move roof water away, grading carries surface water off, interior drains and a pump handle what gets through, and a backup keeps the pump running when the power fails. We handle the plumbing layers and tell you honestly when the answer lies with another trade.

Pump, check valve and drain connections follow the maker's instructions and the standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3.

What to do while the basement is wet

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or the panel may be wet
  • Switch off power to the basement only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water
  • Wear boots and gloves, because floodwater and sewage can carry germs
  • Lift stored items onto shelves and open windows to dry the air
  • Take dated photos for your insurer before you move anything
  • Do not run a vacuum or a box fan in standing water
