When repiping is the right call

One pinhole leak is a repair. Several leaks in a year, brown water from every tap or pressure that drops when two fixtures run are signs the pipe is wearing out. Repairing one section often stresses the next, and the bills add up.

Repiping replaces the supply lines with new pipe, usually copper or PEX. It is a larger project than a repair, but it ends the cycle of leaks and restores flow.

Low pressure

Galvanized pipe corrodes inside and narrows until little water gets through.

Rusty or brown water

Rust flakes from the inside of old steel lines.

Repeated pinholes

A second or third leak in the same line means the whole run is thin.

Discolored pipes

Green copper or flaking galvanized pipe shows corrosion outside too.

Perry Township has 12,577 housing units with a median owner-occupied value of about 152,000 dollars, which means many owners are weighing a repipe against other repairs.

Ohio's plumbing code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, governs materials and methods. The Massillon Building Department registers plumbing contractors and handles permits in the city, and we tell you when your job needs one.

Living through a repipe

A repipe is disruptive for a few days, and a clear plan makes it easier. We tell you which fixtures will be off and for how long, and we try to restore water at the end of each day. Plan for dust near access holes, and move furniture away from the walls where we will work.

Fill a few jugs for drinking and cooking on the days the main is closed. Pets and children should be kept away from open walls and tools. When we finish, we flush the new lines, test every fixture and check the pressure, so you know the system works before we leave.

New supply lines follow the material and sizing standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, and the pipe maker's instructions for joints and support.
