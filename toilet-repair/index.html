A toilet overflow does not wait

If water is rising, lift the tank lid, push the flapper down and shut off the supply valve behind the bowl by turning it clockwise. That stops the flow. Then mop up quickly, because water that reaches a subfloor soaks in and causes damage.

If the toilet clogs again after you clear it, the problem may be deeper than the trap. Other fixtures backing up together mean the issue is in the branch line or the main.

Overflow or slow flush

A partial clog in the trap or the line slows the bowl.

Constant running

A worn flapper or fill valve leaks water into the bowl and raises the bill.

Rocking base

A loose bowl breaks the wax seal and can leak onto the floor.

Water at the base

A bad wax ring or a cracked bowl leaks when the toilet flushes.

What a toilet repair involves

1

Stop the water

We close the supply and protect the floor.

2

Find the fault

We test the flapper, fill valve, fill line and the seal at the floor.

3

Repair or reset

We replace worn parts or pull the toilet to reset it on a new wax ring.

4

Test flushes

We run several flushes and check for leaks at every joint.

If an overflow soaked a floor, your homeowner's policy may cover the water damage. We can document what we found.

Toilet problems in older Massillon homes

Older Massillon houses often have toilets set over cast iron or brass flanges that have corroded. A rocking toilet in a house this old may sit on a flange that has rusted or loosened from the subfloor. Replacing a wax ring will not hold if the flange is broken.

If the toilet clogs repeatedly in a house with a basement floor drain that also backs up, call us for sewer line cleaning. A toilet is the highest fixture on the line, and it shows the first sign of a downstream problem.

A reset toilet is sealed and anchored according to the maker's instructions and the fixture standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3.
