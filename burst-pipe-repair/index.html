A burst pipe: stop the water first

Find the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line enters the basement or crawlspace, and turn it clockwise until it stops. Then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes. If water is near the electrical panel or outlets, switch off power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Call us after the water is off. Take photos of the damage before you move anything, because your insurer will ask for them. Move rugs and valuables off wet floors and open windows if it is safe to ventilate.

A sudden rush or hiss

Water flowing inside a wall or ceiling after a cold night.

No water from one tap

A frozen section of line blocks flow. It may split as it thaws.

Bulging or frost on a pipe

A bulge means ice inside is expanding the pipe.

A wet ceiling or floor

Water finds the lowest point and shows up away from the break.

What burst pipe repair costs and what changes it

You hear a clear price before work starts. A single split in an accessible pipe is a very different job from multiple splits behind finished walls.

The factors:

Number of breaksFrozen pipes often split in several places.
LocationOpen basement joists are easy. Finished drywall takes more work.
Pipe materialCopper, PEX and galvanized each need different fittings.
Water damage workDrying and restoration are separate from the plumbing repair.
Time of dayNight, weekend and holiday visits can cost more.

Sudden pipe bursts are commonly covered by homeowner's insurance for the water damage. Call your insurer and ask. We can supply a written description of the failure.

Why Massillon pipes burst in winter

Northeast Ohio winters bring hard cold snaps that reach pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawlspaces and basements with drafty rim joists. Many Massillon homes built before 1970, and Jackson Township reports 34 percent of its housing built before 1970, have supply lines in places where cold air gets in.

Before a freeze, insulate exposed pipes, seal gaps around the rim joist and keep the heat on in the house even if you travel. If the pipe is old galvanized steel, a split often means other sections are weak too, and our repiping page explains when replacing the run beats patching it.

Replacement pipe and fittings follow the material standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, and are pressure tested before we close the work.
