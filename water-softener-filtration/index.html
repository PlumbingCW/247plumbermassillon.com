What hard water does to a house

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Those minerals leave scale inside water heaters, on faucet aerators and on shower glass. They shorten appliance life, force a water heater to work harder and leave soap film on skin and dishes.

A water softener swaps those minerals for sodium or potassium. A filter removes other things, such as chlorine taste, sediment or certain contaminants. Many homes need one, not the other, and the right answer starts with a test.

White crust on fixtures

Spots on glasses

Stiff laundry and dry skin

Short heater life

Sediment on the tank bottom shortens life and wastes energy.

Massillon water: test, do not guess

Most Massillon addresses are served by Aqua Ohio's Massillon Division, which draws from ten wells in a sand and gravel aquifer in the Tuscarawas Valley and buys some water from North Canton. In Perry Township, the name on your bill tells you whether Aqua Ohio or the City of Canton serves you.

The utility's annual report does not give a hardness number we can quote, so we do not. Ask the utility, or let us test your tap. That tells you whether a softener makes sense, and protects you from buying equipment you do not need. Aqua Ohio also reported that its 2024 lead and copper sampling of 33 homes found none above the action level.

Living with a softener or filter

Treatment systems need small, regular care. A softener needs salt, and the brine tank should be checked every month or two. A filter cartridge needs replacing on schedule, and a carbon filter loses effect when it is overdue. Write the dates on the unit or on a calendar.

If your water changes, such as a new taste or a stain on fixtures, tell us. A softener with a resin bed that has worn out stops softening, and an iron problem may need a different filter than the one you have.

Treatment equipment is installed according to the maker's instructions, with a bypass and an air gap on the drain as the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, expects.
