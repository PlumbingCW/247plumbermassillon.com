A jammed or leaking disposal in a busy kitchen

A humming disposal that does not spin is jammed. Switch it off at the wall, do not put your hand in, and use a wrench in the bottom hex socket to free it. If that fails, or if the unit is leaking from the housing, call us.

A disposal that works but drains slowly may be connected to a clogged line. Running it harder will not help, and grease poured through it ends up further down the pipe where it hardens.

Humming but not spinning

Something is jamming the flywheel, or the motor has seized.

Water under the sink

A cracked housing, a bad gasket or a loose drain line leaks into the cabinet.

Foul smell

Food buildup on the walls and the splash guard causes odors.

Backs up into the other sink

A double sink shares a trap, and a clog past the disposal shows up in both bowls.

What goes down the drain and what should not

Disposals handle soft food scraps. They do not handle bones, stringy vegetables, coffee grounds in volume, pasta that swells, or grease. Those clog the trap and the line past it, which is a common cause of kitchen backups in older Massillon homes with small drain lines.

If the kitchen line is old cast iron, grease from years of use may have narrowed it. A disposal repair visit often turns into a drain job, and in that case we explain the options, from a cable to hydro jetting.

A disposal is wired and drained according to the maker's instructions and the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, which sets standards for the trap and the drain connection.
