A failed tank is a flood waiting to happen

When the shell of a tank rusts through, water leaves faster than a floor drain can take it. If the base of the heater is wet, shut off the cold water supply and the power or gas, and call us. Replacement is the only fix for a shell leak, and speed matters because the water keeps flowing until the tank is empty.

Even without a leak, a heater past its working life can go without warning. Planning a replacement before it fails lets you pick the size and fuel type calmly instead of at 2 a.m.

Water around the base

Moisture at the bottom of the tank that does not come from a fitting means the shell has failed.

Rusty hot water

Brown or red water from the hot tap only, and not the cold, points to corrosion inside the tank.

Heater age near ten years

The label's serial number shows the date. Many tanks fail between eight and twelve years.

Repeated repairs

When parts keep failing, the cost of the next repair starts to approach a new unit.

Choosing a replacement: tank, tankless or heat pump

Most Massillon homes swap tank for tank, because the gas line, vent and water connections are already in place. Tankless units save floor space and never run out of hot water, but they cost more up front and often need a larger gas line. Heat pump models cost less to run and need room and a drain.

OptionStrengthWatch for
Gas tankFamiliar, fast recovery, lower fuel cost than electric resistanceNeeds a safe vent and a working gas supply
Electric tankSimple install, no ventingHigher running cost, needs the right circuit
TanklessEndless hot water, small footprintHigher install cost, may need a bigger gas line
Heat pump tankLowest running costNeeds space, airflow and a condensate drain

Your plumber recommends an option after seeing your setup.

What replacement costs and what changes it

You hear a clear price before work starts. A direct swap and a conversion to tankless are very different jobs, so we quote after we see the existing setup.

These factors move the price:

Unit type and sizeA fifty gallon gas tank and a tankless unit have different equipment costs.
Fuel and ventingChanging fuel or fixing a failed vent adds materials and labor.
Code updatesAn old install may need an expansion tank, a pan or a new relief line.
AccessStairs, tight closets and long runs add time.
PermitsSome replacements need a permit, which adds a step.

If a failed tank flooded your basement, your homeowner's policy may cover the water damage and not the heater. We can write up what we found for your claim.

Replacement notes for Massillon homes

Many houses in Massillon and Perry Township were built in the 1950s through 1970s, with gas heaters in basements and vents that go back to older chimneys. A new high efficiency tank may need a different vent than the one it replaces, so we check the chimney liner and the vent path before we order a unit.

Plumbing work in the city may need a permit. The Massillon Building Department at One James Duncan Plaza registers plumbing contractors, and we tell you at booking whether your job needs one.

A replacement tank is set to the standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, and to the maker's installation instructions, including the relief valve, the discharge pipe and the vent.

No hot water: repair or replace

When a heater gives no hot water, the cause is often a failed element, thermostat or pilot assembly, and a repair may be all you need. If the tank is leaking from the body, a repair will not hold and replacement is the sound choice.

Tell us the age of the heater and whether the leak is at the tank or at a fitting. That answer decides whether we bring parts or a new unit.
