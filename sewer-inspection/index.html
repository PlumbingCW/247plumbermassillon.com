What a camera inspection shows

A waterproof camera head travels through the line while the plumber watches a live feed. It shows the pipe material, the joints, roots, scale, grease, cracks, sags and breaks. A locator at the surface marks the depth and position of any problem.

That turns guessing into knowing. A line that clogs twice a year could have roots, a low spot or a collapsed joint, and each needs a different fix. Paying for the right repair beats paying for another cable visit.

When to get an inspection

Repeat clogs

A line that fills again after cleaning has a cause the camera can find.

Buying an older house

A pre-purchase scope shows whether the sewer line is sound before you commit.

Before a big project

Check the line before you pour a patio or plant a tree over it.

After a backup

The camera shows whether the failure was a clog or a break.

Massillon lines: age and ownership

Massillon's sewer system includes pipe more than a century old, and the city's first treatment plant dates to 1892. Private laterals vary widely, from clay to cast iron to newer plastic. A camera run shows which you have and what shape it is in.

The city maintains public lines and manholes. The pipe from your house to the connection is generally yours. Buyers of older homes in Massillon and Perry Township benefit from scoping the lateral before closing.

Reading the footage with your plumber

Footage can look alarming to someone who has not seen inside a pipe. Roots look like white hair, scale looks like rough crust, and a belly shows up as standing water in the image. Your plumber explains what each means and how serious it is. A little scale is normal in an old line, and a collapsed section is a real repair.

Ask for a distance measurement for each finding. That tells you where to dig, if digging is needed, and helps a repair crew price the work accurately. Keep the recording with your house records, because a later buyer or an insurer may ask for it.

A camera inspection records the condition of the pipe against the standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, for slope, material and joints.
