When a plumbing failure shuts the doors

A restaurant with a failed floor drain cannot serve food. A retail shop with a dead restroom may be required to close. For a tenant or a property manager, every hour of downtime costs money, so the first step is to stop the damage and the second is to restore service.

Close the nearest shutoff valve, turn off equipment on the affected line, and call. If you lease the space, check your lease for who repairs what and tell your landlord.

Restroom or kitchen backups

Multiple fixtures overflowing together point to a main or branch line.

Loss of hot water

Dishwashing and handwashing depend on it, and many businesses cannot open without it.

Grease trap overflow

Foul odors and slow kitchen drains often mean the trap is full.

Water at the ceiling

A leak above a drop ceiling can drip onto stock, equipment and customers.

What commercial plumbing costs and what changes it

You hear a clear price before work starts. Commercial jobs vary widely in size, so we scope the problem first.

The main factors are:

Building systemsLarger pipes, more fixtures and older risers add complexity.
AccessCeilings, mezzanines and occupied space may limit where we can work.
EquipmentCommercial water heaters, backflow devices and grease traps need specific parts.
DowntimeWork after hours may reduce lost business and may cost more than daytime work.
PermitsCommercial work more often needs a permit and an inspection.

Business property insurance varies on water damage and sewer backup coverage. Ask your agent, and we can supply a written description of the failure.

Massillon businesses on Lincoln Way and downtown

Lincoln Way, the old Lincoln Highway and US 30 corridor, carries a long run of shops, restaurants and service businesses through Massillon and Perry Township. About half the business land use in Perry Township sits along Lincoln Way, so many tenants share buildings and sometimes share lines. A clog in one unit can back up into the next.

Downtown buildings, such as the Gensemer Brothers building that houses the Massillon Museum, may have decades-old piping. Plumbing contractors in the city register with the Massillon Building Department, and commercial work often needs a permit, which we raise with you before we start.

Commercial plumbing follows the standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, and commercial jobs more often involve backflow protection and permit inspections.
