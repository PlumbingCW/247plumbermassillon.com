Sewage in the house means the line has failed or is blocked

A backup that returns after a drain cleaning points to a damaged pipe. Common causes are roots that entered a joint, a pipe that has cracked or sagged, and old cast iron that has flaked apart. In each case the repair is a pipe problem, not a clog.

Stop using water in the house. Every flush and shower pushes more into the line. Keep children and pets out of any floor that has taken sewage, and wear gloves and boots if you must go in.

Backups after cleaning

A clear line that fills again within days usually has a break or a belly.

Wet or sunken ground

A soggy patch or a dip in the yard along the line can mean a break.

Sewer odor

Persistent smell near a drain or in the yard points to a leak.

Roots or gnats

Fine roots in the line and drain flies can both signal a cracked pipe.

Massillon sewers: who owns what

Massillon runs a municipal sewer system with about 17,475 sanitary sewer connections, and city crews maintain the lines and manholes. The oldest parts of the system are more than a century old, and the city's first treatment plant dates to 1892. Your private lateral, the pipe from your house to the connection, is generally the owner's to repair.

In Perry Township the sewer service comes from the Stark County Metropolitan Sewer District, and much of that flow goes to Massillon's plant. About 1,475 septic systems also remain in the township, so confirm whether your home is on a sewer or septic before we plan a repair. Spring flooding in the Tuscarawas Valley has stressed older lines, and a line with a belly collects water in it long after the storm.

Sewer repairs follow the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3, which sets standards for pipe material, joints and slope in a building sewer.
