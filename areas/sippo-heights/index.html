Sippo Heights at a glance

Sippo Heights is a named area in the City of Massillon, in ZIP code 44646. The U.S. Geological Survey place-name record lists it as a populated place inside the city, and public neighborhood data places Lincoln Way East, Wales Road and Sippo Creek in and around it. Streets in the area include Colonial Parkway, Tanglewood Drive and Sippo Boulevard.

The housing is mostly single-family houses. Public data suggests most were built before 1980, with a median year built in the early 1960s, so ranches, split-levels and older traditional homes sit side by side. Boundaries are not officially fixed, so if you are not sure whether your street counts, call and we will confirm.

Homes from the mid-20th century

A house built in the 1960s is old enough that its original water heater has probably been replaced once or twice, and old enough that a bathroom fan or a basement drain may have been added later. None of that means anything is wrong. It means the plumbing has a history, and a short look at the basement tells you most of it.

Water heaters are the most common reason to call. A tank that is leaking at the base, rumbling or no longer keeping up is worth looking at soon. We compare repair with replacement and give you the numbers side by side.

A creek, basements and sump pumps

Sippo Creek runs through the area, and parks along it are part of why people live here. We do not claim that any street takes on water. We do say that a basement near a creek, or any low spot, is a place where a working sump pump is worth having.

If your pump runs constantly, never runs, or makes a grinding sound, call before the next storm. Test it by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching it start. If it does not, tell us what you saw.

Who handles which water and sewer line

Water in Massillon comes from the Aqua Ohio Massillon Division, and the city maintains the public sewer lines and manholes. Your plumber works on the pipe from the connection to your house and everything inside it. If you see water bubbling up in the street, call the utility named on your bill. Repairs on your side follow the standards in the Ohio Plumbing Code, OAC Chapter 4101:3.

If you are not sure whether a backup is in your line or the public line, a camera inspection settles it. We explain what we find and what it means for who pays.
