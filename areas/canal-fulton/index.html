Emergency plumbing in a canal town

Canal Fulton had about 5,325 residents in 2020. The Ohio and Erie Canal was built through here between 1826 and 1828, and the town grew around it. Fulton was platted in 1826, took the name Canal Fulton in 1832, and absorbed Milan and West Fulton in 1853. The Union School dates from 1855 and the Opera House from 1873.

A place with that much history has a lot of old buildings, and old buildings have old pipes. When something fails at night, call. We answer around the clock, book the next appointment and tell you the price before work starts.

City utilities and your side of the line

Canal Fulton operates its own water and sewer system, with the wastewater plant at 5500 Butterbridge Road NW. Responsibility for water and sewer lines is shared between the property owner and the city. In general, the city handles the main and you handle the pipe from the connection to your house, but your property may have different details.

The city also runs a backflow prevention program, which uses an online system for testing records. If you have an irrigation system, a fire line or a business that needs a backflow device, ask the city about testing. We can help with the plumbing side.

Old buildings, mixed pipes, winter freezes

Historic buildings in a canal town often have a mix of cast iron drains, galvanized supply lines and later copper. Each part ages differently. A leak in one section is often a signal about its neighbors, which is why we look at the whole run before we recommend a repair.

Winter freezes hit hardest where pipes run in an exterior wall or an unheated space. If a pipe splits, shut off the main and call us. Our burst pipe repair page explains what to do first, and repiping is worth discussing if the same line fails twice.

What a call to Canal Fulton looks like

Most calls from a canal-era town start with a symptom and no obvious cause. A basement drain gurgles, a faucet runs rusty, or a bathroom ceiling shows a stain. We ask where the water is and what changed, then walk you through the first steps over the phone. If the water is moving, close the nearest valve. If it is not, leave things as they are so the plumber can see the evidence.

We plan each Canal Fulton visit with the right parts on the truck. A building with a mix of copper and old steel needs different fittings from a newer one, so the more you tell us about the age of the pipes, the better we prepare.
