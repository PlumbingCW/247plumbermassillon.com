A neighborhood with its own history

West Massillon began as a separate town. William Henry founded it in 1831 on the west bank of the Tuscarawas River, and it joined Kendal and Massillon when the city incorporated in 1853, according to the City of Massillon. Today it is a residential part of the city west of downtown, in ZIP code 44647.

That history matters when you are choosing a plumber, because the houses here were built over a long stretch of time. Public neighborhood data points to many homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s, with newer houses mixed in. A plumber should look at your actual pipes instead of guessing what is behind the wall.

What we look at in an older West Massillon house

A house that has stood for a century has usually been worked on many times. Some of the pipes may be original, some may be newer copper or plastic, and the join between the two is where many leaks start. We trace the line from the meter to the fixture and tell you what we find, so you can decide with the facts in front of you.

We do not assume a material from the age of the house. If you want to know what you have, ask for a look at the main shutoff and the lines near it. The answer shapes the choice between a small repair and a repiping quote.

  • Find the main shutoff valve and make sure it turns
  • Note any pipe that is a different color or size than its neighbors
  • Look under sinks and around the water heater for stains or drips
  • Write down which fixtures run slow or gurgle

Water, sewer and who owns which pipe

Water service in Massillon comes from the Aqua Ohio Massillon Division, which draws from wells in the Tuscarawas Valley and buys additional water from North Canton. Sewer service is run by the city, which maintains the public lines and manholes. The city counts 17,475 sewer connections, and the system is more than 100 years old.

As a rule, the utility owns the main in the street and you own the pipe from the connection to your house. If sewage backs up into a basement drain, a camera inspection shows whether the blockage is in your line or past your property line. Our sewer inspection visit gives you that answer, and a drain cleaning visit clears a clog in your own line.

Flooding history and what it means for your basement

The 1913 flood hit the whole Tuscarawas valley, and the city built levees and viaducts between 1939 and 1951 to protect the business district. Those works are a reminder that this is river country. They do not tell you the condition of your own basement, and we do not claim that a given street floods.

If your basement takes on water or a floor drain backs up in heavy rain, call and describe what you see. We will tell you whether the cause looks like a failed sump pump, a sewer line problem or water coming in through the wall.
